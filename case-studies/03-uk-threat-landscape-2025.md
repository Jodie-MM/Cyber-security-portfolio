# 03: The UK threat picture in 2025, and what I'd recommend

**The question:** What does the official evidence say about cyber threats to UK organisations right now, and what should they do about it?

**Main source:** NCSC Annual Review 2025 (covering September 2024 to August 2025)

---

## What the evidence says

- **Serious incidents more than doubled.** The NCSC handled 204 nationally significant incidents, up about 130% on the year before. That's roughly four a week.
- **Ransomware is the most immediate risk.** The attacks on Marks & Spencer, the Co-op and Jaguar Land Rover were named as reminders of the real-world cost.
- **AI is helping attackers.** Researchers have seen it used for fully automated spear-phishing and for automating stages of an attack after the attacker gets in.
- **State-linked threats continue,** especially from China, Russia, Iran and North Korea.

## My recommendations

| Finding | Recommendation | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| Attackers look for flaws in code and configuration | Patch quickly, prioritising anything flagged by the NCSC or CISA | Stops the easiest way in |
| Ransomware stops operations | Offline backups that have been tested by actually restoring from them | You can recover without paying |
| Stolen logins | MFA everywhere and limited admin accounts | A stolen password alone isn't enough to get in |
| AI-written phishing | Regular phishing training and an easy "report it" button | Staff become a sensor rather than the weak point |
| Attacks through suppliers | Check suppliers' security before giving them access | Your data is only as safe as their security |
| Incidents will happen | An incident response plan that has been tested and has board support | Everyone knows their role when it happens |

---

## What I took from this

Most of these recommendations are basic security practice. Even with AI and state-linked groups in the picture, I think doing the basics consistently would stop a large share of these attacks. That's my view; it isn't a figure from the review.
